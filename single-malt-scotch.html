How to read this list

“Single malt” means whisky made from malted barley at one distillery. Regions give a rough guide to style, not a guarantee.

Gentle & fruity

Highland and Speyside malts such as Glenmorangie, Balvenie, and Glenfiddich: honey, orchard fruit, vanilla, soft oak.

Rich & sherried

Aberlour, Dalmore, and Macallan: dried fruit, orange, chocolate, and baking spice from sherry-cask maturation.

Coastal & smoky

Oban, Talisker, and the Islay malts: sea salt, pepper, and peat smoke, from gentle to intense.

West Highlands · 43% ABV

A beautifully balanced West Highland malt. Orange peel, sea salt, and gentle smoke, followed by honey, ripe fruit, malt, and a touch of spice. Creamy texture and a long, warming finish with oak and dried fruit. Complexity without overwhelming peat.

A classic, gentle Speyside malt bottled by Justerini & Brooks from the 1984 distilling season. Light and elegant: malt, honey, soft orchard fruit, a touch of dried grass and vanilla, with a clean, easy finish. Knockando is known for dating its bottles by season rather than by batch.

Tasting tips

Getting more from every pour

Glassware & pour

Use a tulip-shaped glass such as a Glencairn to concentrate aromas. A small pour of about 1 to 1.5 oz is plenty.

Add water carefully

A few drops can open higher-proof whiskies like Uigeadail. Taste neat first, then adjust.

Go slowly

Nose first, sip small, and let it rest on the palate. Take notes and compare bottles side by side.

Drink responsibly

Enjoy in moderation, never drink and drive, and only if you are of legal drinking age.
