Security leadership

Cybersecurity Tabletop Exercises

Practice decisions before an incident, identify gaps, and turn the lessons into action.

Set up a useful exercise

  1. Pick one objective, such as deciding who can authorize isolation of a critical system.
  2. Invite decision-makers from security, IT, legal, communications and affected business teams. Assign a facilitator and a note-taker.
  3. Set ground rules: use the exercise for learning, not blame; do not make real production changes.
  4. Run a 45–90 minute discussion with timed scenario updates, then capture owners and due dates for improvements.

Three scenarios to try

Ransomware interrupts a key service

Backups may be affected and a restoration estimate is uncertain. Who declares an incident, approves containment and decides which service returns first? What evidence and communications must be preserved?

Compromised administrator account

An unfamiliar sign-in is followed by privilege changes. Who can disable access, review logs and coordinate with the identity provider? How will you regain control if the primary admin is unavailable?

Third-party disruption

A critical provider reports a breach but has limited facts. Who contacts the provider, assesses exposure and makes the decision to activate a workaround?

Turn discussion into improvement

Record decisions, assumptions, dependencies and unresolved questions. Assign each gap an owner and review date; test revised procedures in a follow-up exercise. Involve counsel in notification decisions and follow applicable requirements.
