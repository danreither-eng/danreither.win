How to read this list

Styles and ABV vary by brewery and season; check the can or carton for the exact details.

Belgian & German classics

Leffe Blonde and Tucher Dunkles Hefe Weizen: yeast-driven fruit and spice.

Lagers

2SP Delco Lager and Zero Gravity Märzen: clean, crisp and malt-forward.

Local & seasonal

Delco Lager and the Oktoberfest Märzen reward drinking fresh and in season.

The lineup

Four beers worth pouring

01

Leffe Blonde

Belgium · abbey blonde ale · about 6.6% ABV

A classic Belgian abbey-style blonde. Golden and lightly sweet with banana, clove, honey and a touch of citrus, finishing smooth with gentle bitterness.

Best for: a gateway into Belgian beer; chicken and seafood

02

2SP Delco Lager

Aston, Pennsylvania · lager

A crisp, clean local lager from 2SP Brewing in Delaware County. Light malt sweetness, a snappy finish and plenty of drinkability.

Best for: backyard cookouts and supporting local brewers

03

Tucher Dunkles Hefe Weizen

Bavaria, Germany · dark wheat beer

A dark, unfiltered Bavarian wheat beer. Banana and clove from the yeast meet toasted bread, caramel and a hint of chocolate with a soft, creamy body.

Best for: pretzels, sausages, and cooler evenings

04

Zero Gravity Oktoberfest Märzen Lager

Burlington, Vermont · Märzen lager

A seasonal take on the traditional Märzen. Toasty malt, light caramel and bready sweetness, with a clean, balanced finish.

Best for: autumn gatherings and grilled sausages

Serving tips

Getting more from every pour

Use the right glass

Wheat beers and abbey ales open up in a tall, curved glass that holds the foam.

Serve cool, not frozen

Lagers around 38 to 45°F; Belgian ales and wheat beers a little warmer.

Drink fresh

Check dates, especially on seasonal and hop-driven styles.

Drink responsibly

Enjoy in moderation, never drink and drive, and only if you are of legal drinking age.
