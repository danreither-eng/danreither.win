Before you go

Choose the right adventure

Ratings are subjective editorial picks for scenery and overall experience, not safety or difficulty scores. Five is exceptional; three is a worthwhile stop. Trail conditions, closures, shuttle service, entrance reservations and permits can change. Check the linked land manager before leaving, carry a map and tell someone your plan.

Arches National Park · Utah

Explore sandstone fins and natural arches near Moab. Most routes offer little shade; sandstone and exposed ledges can be slippery. Official hiking guide.

A maze of sandstone fins and narrow passages without a maintained trail. Join a ranger-led hike or obtain a self-guided permit; prepare to navigate, squeeze through gaps, and scramble.

Zion National Park · Utah

Steep canyon walls, river walks, and exposed viewpoints. Check shuttle schedules, flash-flood forecasts, closures, and current water advisories.

Angels Landing

Scenery: 5/5 · Strenuous and exposed

A spectacular ridge with steep drop-offs and chains. A permit is required beyond Scout Lookout; do not attempt it in unsafe weather or if exposure exceeds your comfort.

The Narrows

Scenery: 5/5 · Difficult river hiking

Walk upstream in the Virgin River between towering canyon walls. Check flash-flood potential and closures; never drink river water because filters do not reliably remove cyanotoxins. Top-down trips require a permit.

Recommended hiking gear

The best gear fits you and the route. Break in footwear before a major hike, test your pack, and adapt this list to weather and remoteness.

Footwear and socks

Choose well-fitting trail runners or hiking boots with dependable grip; boots may suit people who prefer ankle coverage or carry heavier loads. Pair with moisture-wicking wool or synthetic socks, and carry a spare pair.

How to choose hiking footwear →

Pack and hydration

A comfortable daypack with room for layers and emergency supplies, plus bottles or a reservoir, is a good starting point. Calculate water for the route, heat and person; verify refill availability and carry extra. Electrolytes and salty food can help replace losses.

NPS ten essentials →

Sun and weather protection

Bring a broad-brim hat, sunglasses, sunscreen, and breathable sun-protective clothing. Pack a rain shell and an insulating layer even when the forecast looks favorable.

NPS ten essentials →

Navigation and emergency kit

Carry an offline map and a paper map and compass you know how to use; bring a headlamp with spare batteries, first-aid and blister supplies, repair tool, emergency shelter, extra food, and a fire starter where permitted. A satellite communicator is valuable beyond cell coverage, but is no guarantee of rescue.

NPS ten essentials →

Poles and river gear

Trekking poles help with uneven ground and descents. For the Narrows, consider purpose-built river footwear and a sturdy staff; cold water may require additional protective layers. Rentals and gear do not make a flood-prone canyon safe.

Narrows safety guidance →

Plan for the place

Carry bear spray where recommended and know local rules; use traction where ice is present. Do not rely on ordinary water filters for Zion cyanotoxins. Turn around for storms, unsafe crossings, illness or an unreliable route.
